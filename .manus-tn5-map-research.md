# TN-5 Interactive Map Research — Aug. 15, 2026

## Verified district source

The Tennessee Secretary of State confirms that the General Assembly adopted revised congressional boundaries in May 2026 and directs viewers to the Comptroller’s official district-map resources.[1] The Comptroller provides the official 2026 congressional map and a dedicated 5th District PDF.[2]

## County coverage for the map

WPLN lists the 2026 TN-5 county coverage as **Williamson, Maury, Lewis, Hickman, Montgomery, Humphreys, Houston, Stewart, Benton, Henry, Weakley, Obion, Lake, Dyer, Lauderdale, Tipton, and Shelby**.[3] Reporting emphasizes that several are only partly included; map details and an address lookup—not a county label alone—control a voter’s actual district assignment.[1][4]

## County-intelligence framing

Tennessee Lookout describes the district as an upside-down horseshoe spanning parts of 17 counties, connecting rural agricultural counties, lower-income South Memphis communities, and affluent Williamson County.[5] The interactive panel should therefore distinguish public district context from campaign assumptions and avoid implying that an entire county is uniformly inside the district or politically homogeneous.

NewsChannel 5 similarly describes the Middle and West Tennessee path: western Williamson, half of Maury, Lewis, Hickman, Humphreys, Houston, Stewart, part of north Montgomery, Benton, Henry, Weakley, Obion, Lake, Dyer, Lauderdale, part of Tipton, and downtown Memphis.[4]

## Opponent context

Chaz Molder’s campaign website frames his profile around Columbia mayoral experience, local government, balanced budgets, infrastructure, education, corruption, and rising costs. Its claims must be presented as campaign-stated positions until independently verified.[6] The FEC reported $1,482,195.91 in Molder cash on hand through July 17, 2026; that filing date should always accompany the figure.[7]
